# Series and parallel circuits and electrical power

In a series circuit there is one path, so the current is the same through every component.
The potential differences across the components add to the supply potential difference.
For resistors in series:

`R_total = R_1 + R_2 + ...`

In a parallel circuit each branch has the same potential difference as the supply.
Current divides between branches, and the branch currents add to the total current.
Adding another parallel path reduces total resistance, so total parallel resistance is less than the resistance of any individual branch.
Parallel resistances must not be added as though they were in series.

Electrical power is the rate of energy transfer, measured in watts (`W`):

`P = VI`

For a resistive component, combining this with `V = IR` gives:

`P = I^2 R`

Potential difference `V` is in volts, current `I` in amperes, and resistance `R` in ohms.
Power is a rate, not an amount of energy.

Battery cells can be arranged in series to build potential difference or in parallel to provide parallel paths.
In light strings, series and parallel arrangements differ because a break in the only series path stops current everywhere, while other parallel branches can remain complete.
