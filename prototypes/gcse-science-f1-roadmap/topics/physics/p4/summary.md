# Newton's three laws

A resultant force is the vector sum of all forces acting on one object.
Choose a positive direction before adding one-dimensional forces.

## First law

If the resultant force on an object is zero, an object at rest remains at rest and a moving object continues at constant velocity.
Constant velocity does not mean that no forces act.
It means that the forces balance to give zero resultant force.

## Second law

For constant mass, resultant force, mass and acceleration are related by:

`F = ma`

Use force in newtons, `N`, mass in kilograms, `kg`, and acceleration in metres per second squared, `m/s^2`.
Acceleration points in the direction of the resultant force.
Convert mass to kilograms before substitution and preserve the chosen signs.

## Third law

When object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an equal-magnitude force in the opposite direction on object A.
The two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel on one object's free-body diagram.
Balanced forces instead act on the same object and have zero vector sum.

The tyre-track interaction on a corner is a third-law pair because the tyre pushes sideways on the track and the track pushes back on the tyre.
Without a sideways resultant force, the car's direction would not change.
Nail-gun recoil can illustrate the third law, but it is an explanation only and not an instruction to handle or operate the tool.
