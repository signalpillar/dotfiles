# Planning physics practicals

A useful plan asks a clear question and changes only the **independent variable**.
The **dependent variable** is measured, while **control variables** are kept as constant as practical so that the comparison is fair.
A control variable is a condition to manage, not a control group.

## Choosing measurements

Choose an instrument that measures the required quantity over a suitable range and with useful resolution.
Resolution describes the smallest scale step or change that can be read, but it is not the whole uncertainty.
Uncertainty also depends on the instrument, the way it is read, variation in repeated results, and the method.
Record units and use only as much precision as the measurements justify.

## Repeats and quality

**Repetition** means taking further readings or repeating the method.
Repeated readings can reveal variation, help identify an unusual result, and support a representative value and an estimate of spread.
**Repeatability** describes agreement when measurements are made again under the same conditions.
**Reproducibility** asks whether a result can be obtained under changed conditions, such as by a different person or with different equipment.
Repeating readings once does not by itself establish reproducibility.

## Evaluating a method

Evaluate whether the variables were controlled, the instruments and intervals were suitable, enough readings were taken, uncertainty was considered, and the conclusion is supported by the data.
Suggest a specific improvement linked to a weakness rather than simply saying to be more careful.
Any school practical must use a teacher-approved method, suitable risk controls, and qualified supervision; planning evidence does not justify handling hazardous apparatus independently.
