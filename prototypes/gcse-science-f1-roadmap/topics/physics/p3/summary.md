# Acceleration and velocity-time graphs

Velocity includes direction, so choose a positive direction before using signs.
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity:

`a = (v - u) / t`

Here `u` is initial velocity in `m/s`, `v` is final velocity in `m/s`, `t` is time in `s`, and `a` is acceleration in `m/s^2`.
A negative acceleration means acceleration in the chosen negative direction.
If an object moving in the positive direction slows down, either report the signed acceleration or report the positive magnitude as deceleration, but do not mix the two conventions.

## Velocity-time graphs

Time is on the horizontal axis and velocity is on the vertical axis.
Graph height gives velocity, not acceleration.

The gradient gives acceleration:

`acceleration = change in velocity / change in time`

The signed area between the graph and the time axis gives displacement.
Area above the time axis is positive and area below it is negative when those signs match the chosen direction convention.
Adding signed areas gives net displacement, not necessarily total distance travelled.

## Constant acceleration

For straight-line motion with constant acceleration:

`v^2 = u^2 + 2as`

Here `s` is signed displacement in metres.
Choose signs consistently, convert units before substitution, and check that both sides have units of `m^2/s^2`.
The squared velocity equation may produce a speed magnitude, so use the physical situation and direction convention when interpreting direction.

The permitted car example connects rapid speeding up with much larger roughly `5g` braking, while the cordless-drill example can be represented by sections that spin up and coast down.
