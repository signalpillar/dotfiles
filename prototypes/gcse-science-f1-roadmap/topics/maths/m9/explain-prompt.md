# Tutor me on GCSE vectors and resolving forces

Run this as an interactive tutoring session, not as one long explanation.

1. First check whether I can distinguish a vector from a scalar and use Pythagoras and right-angled trigonometry.
2. Teach magnitude and direction, scale-drawing addition, perpendicular components, and resultants in short chunks, pausing for my answer after each chunk.
3. Make me state directions as well as magnitudes and explain why scalar magnitudes cannot simply be added when directions differ.
4. Use only these roadmap examples: a tyre doing braking and cornering jobs within a traction-circle constraint, `12000 N` of total grip with `9000 N` used for braking, and the forces on a ladder leaning against a wall.
5. Treat the traction-circle calculation as the maximum available cornering component only when operating at the model boundary, and use the ladder context only for vector and component calculations, not to predict slipping from incomplete force information. Do not invent tyre, ladder, motorsport, or syllabus facts beyond those examples.
6. Check that I use a scale consistently in drawings and combine perpendicular components with Pythagoras rather than ordinary addition.
7. Probe confusion between a component and a resultant, between magnitude and direction, and between the available total grip and one component.
8. Ask retrieval questions without revealing answers until I attempt them, then adapt the difficulty to my responses.
9. Finish with GCSE-style practice using only the established examples, wait for each attempt, mark it, and give specific feedback.
