# Particle model and changes of state

The particle model represents a substance as many tiny particles.
The diagrams are simplified models: the drawn circles are not literal pictures or to scale, and heating changes particle motion and spacing rather than making each particle expand.

In a solid, particles are closely packed in fixed positions and vibrate.
In a liquid, particles remain close but move past one another.
In a gas, particles are much farther apart and move rapidly in random directions.

Melting and freezing change a substance between solid and liquid.
Evaporation or boiling changes liquid to gas, while condensation changes gas to liquid.
These are physical changes: the substance does not become a new substance, and its particles do not disappear.

Mass is conserved during a change of state in a closed system because the same particles remain present.
If gas escapes from an open system, the measured mass left behind can fall, but that does not mean particles were destroyed.
