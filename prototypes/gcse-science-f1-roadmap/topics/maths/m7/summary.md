## Area under a graph

The area between a graph and its horizontal axis can represent a quantity determined by the two axis variables.
This is not automatically an ordinary geometric area, so multiply the axis units to identify what the result means.

- Split a straight-sided region into rectangles and triangles, calculate each area, and add them.
- For a curve, count grid squares or use narrow rectangles to estimate the area.
- Keep the scale and units in every calculation.

On a velocity-time graph, signed area gives displacement.
When velocity remains non-negative, this is also the distance travelled; if the graph crosses below the axis, use the magnitudes of all regions to find total distance.
For a car accelerating uniformly from `0 m/s` to `80 m/s` in `4 s`, the region is a triangle, so the distance is `0.5 x 4 x 80 = 160 m`.
Splitting an odd-shaped wall into rectangles and triangles uses the same decomposition method, but there the result is geometric area.
