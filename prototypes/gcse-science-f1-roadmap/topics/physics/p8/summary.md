# Moments, levers and centre of mass

A moment is the turning effect of a force about a pivot:

`moment = force x perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force's line of action`

Moment is measured in newton metres (`N m`).
The perpendicular distance must be used, not a sloping distance.
Choose and state a clockwise or anticlockwise sign convention when adding moments.

For an object in rotational equilibrium, total clockwise moment equals total anticlockwise moment about the same pivot.
Levers increase a moment by increasing the perpendicular distance, as with a long spanner or wheelbarrow.

The centre of mass is the point through which the object's weight can be treated as acting.
An object remains stable while the vertical line through its centre of mass stays within its base; it tends to topple when that line passes outside.
A lower centre of mass can improve stability, but it is not the pivot and does not alone determine every load change in a vehicle.
