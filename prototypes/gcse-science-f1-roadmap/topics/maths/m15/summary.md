## Sampling, scatter graphs and correlation

A representative sample reflects the important features of the population being studied.
A sample chosen only from an easy or convenient location may be biased, and increasing its size does not remove that bias.

A scatter graph plots paired values for two variables.
An upward pattern shows positive correlation, a downward pattern shows negative correlation, and no clear pattern suggests little or no correlation.

Correlation describes an association, not proof that one variable causes the other.
A confounding variable can affect both measured variables and provide another explanation for the observed pattern.
