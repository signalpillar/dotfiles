## Ratio and proportion

A ratio compares quantities in a stated order, so `a:b` is not interchangeable with `b:a`.
Equivalent ratios are made by multiplying or dividing every part by the same scale factor.

For a part-to-part ratio, the parts are compared with each other.
For a part-to-whole fraction, add all ratio parts first and compare the required part with that total.
This distinction matters for a `58:42` brake bias because the front share is `58 / (58 + 42)` of the total.

In direct proportion, one quantity has the form `y = kx`, so `y / x` stays constant.
In inverse proportion, one quantity has the form `y = k / x`, so the product `xy` stays constant.
Always keep units consistent before applying a ratio or scale factor.
