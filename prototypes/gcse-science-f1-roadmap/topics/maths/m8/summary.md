## Pythagoras and trigonometry

For a right-angled triangle, Pythagoras gives `a^2 + b^2 = c^2`, where `c` is the hypotenuse opposite the right angle.
Use it when two side lengths are known and the third is required.

For an angle `theta` in a right-angled triangle:

- `sin(theta) = opposite / hypotenuse`.
- `cos(theta) = adjacent / hypotenuse`.
- `tan(theta) = opposite / adjacent`.

To find an unknown angle, form the appropriate side ratio and use `sin^-1`, `cos^-1`, or `tan^-1` on a calculator.
Label the sides relative to the chosen angle before selecting a ratio, and keep a calculator in degree mode when angles are given in degrees.
A `3, 4, 5` triangle satisfies Pythagoras, so it can be used to check a square corner.
