# Reflection and refraction of light

A ray diagram is a geometric model that represents the direction of light with thin straight lines and arrowheads.
A ray is not a literal light path with physical thickness.
At a surface, draw a normal perpendicular to the surface at the point where the ray meets it.

For reflection, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
Both angles are measured between the ray and the normal, not between the ray and the surface.

Refraction occurs when light crosses a boundary and its speed changes.
If light slows, the ray bends towards the normal; if it speeds up, it bends away from the normal.
These directions must be decided from the relative speeds in the two materials, not memorised without a condition.
If a ray travelling along the normal changes speed at a boundary, it still does not change direction.

Refraction can make a tile under water appear displaced.
Transparent layers in a visor or camera cover can shift or distort the apparent position of a braking marker depending on their shape and refractive index.
