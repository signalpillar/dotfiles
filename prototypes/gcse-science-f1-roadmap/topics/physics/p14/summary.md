# Thermal physics and specific heat capacity

Temperature measures how hot or cold an object is; it is not the same as the object's total internal energy.
When there is no change of state, the energy transferred is `E = mc x change in temperature`, where `m` is mass in kilograms and `c` is specific heat capacity in joules per kilogram per degree Celsius.
Use `change in temperature = final temperature - initial temperature` and check that the answer is in joules.

During a change of state, transferred energy changes particle arrangement rather than temperature.
The relationship is `E = mL`, where `L` is specific latent heat in joules per kilogram.
Do not apply `E = mc x change in temperature` across the constant-temperature part of a state change.

Conduction transfers energy through particle interactions and is usually faster in metals than in wood.
This is why equal-temperature metal and wooden handles can feel different: the metal transfers energy away from a warm hand faster, but it is not necessarily colder.
Convection transfers energy by the bulk movement of liquids or gases, not solids.
Radiation transfers energy by electromagnetic waves and needs no medium.

Carbon brake discs and tyres illustrate why both energy transfer and working temperature matter, but the near-1000 C brake-disc figure does not imply that every component has the same working temperature.
