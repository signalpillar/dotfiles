# Magnetism, the motor effect and generators

A magnetic field is a region where a magnetic material, magnet, or current-carrying conductor can experience a force.
Field lines represent field direction and relative strength; they are a model, not physical threads.

A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field can experience a force: the motor effect.
When the conductor is perpendicular to the field:

`F = BIl`

Force `F` is in newtons (`N`), magnetic flux density `B` in teslas (`T`), current `I` in amperes (`A`), and conductor length `l` in metres (`m`).
Fleming's left-hand rule links three mutually perpendicular directions: field, conventional current, and force.
Reversing either current or field reverses the force.

Electromagnetic induction occurs when the magnetic flux linking a conductor changes, producing an induced potential difference.
Current flows only if there is a complete conducting path.
A stationary magnet and stationary coil do not produce continuous induction merely because a magnetic field exists.

A motor uses current in a magnetic field to produce force and movement.
A generator uses relative movement or another changing magnetic field to induce a potential difference.
The MGU-K illustrates one machine operating in generator mode under braking and motor mode when adding power; the effects must not be confused.
A cordless drill motor and a wind-up torch provide the same motor-versus-generator contrast.
