# Tutor me on GCSE surface area to volume ratio

Run this as an interactive tutoring session, not as one long explanation.

1. First check whether I can calculate the area of a square, the surface area of a cube, the volume of a cube, and a ratio.
2. Teach surface area, volume, and `surface area / volume` in short chunks, pausing for my answer after each chunk.
3. Make me compare how area and volume change when equal shapes scale up, then connect the ratio to heat or mass transfer.
4. Use only these roadmap examples: `2 cm` and `4 cm` cubes, radiator fins, brake-disc holes, amplifier heatsink fins, and a thin chip compared with a thick one.
5. Keep the brake-disc example to the established facts that holes can affect mass, airflow, and gas escape and therefore have more than one purpose.
6. Do not invent engineering, cooking, or syllabus facts beyond those examples.
7. Probe mistakes such as dividing volume by area, omitting faces, mixing units, or assuming surface area and volume grow by the same factor.
8. Ask retrieval questions without revealing answers until I attempt them, then adapt the difficulty to my responses.
9. Finish with GCSE-style practice using only the established examples, wait for each attempt, mark it, and give specific feedback.
