# Waves and sound

A wave transfers energy through a disturbance without transporting matter overall.
In a transverse wave, oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of travel; in a longitudinal wave, oscillations are parallel.
Sound in air is a longitudinal mechanical wave and needs a medium, so it cannot travel through a vacuum.

Wave speed is related to frequency and wavelength by `v = f x wavelength`, where `v` is in metres per second, `f` is in hertz, and wavelength is in metres.
Frequency is the number of complete waves passing a point each second; wavelength is the distance between matching points on adjacent waves.
For sound in the same medium, a higher frequency means a higher pitch, not a greater wave speed.

At a boundary, some wave energy can be reflected and some can be absorbed.
An echo is a reflected sound pulse.
If a pulse travels to an object and back, the measured echo time covers twice the one-way distance.
The particles of a medium oscillate locally as a mechanical wave passes; they do not travel all the way with the wave.
