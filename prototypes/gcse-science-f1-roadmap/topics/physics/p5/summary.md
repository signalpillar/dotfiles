# Momentum and impulse

Momentum is the product of mass and velocity:

`p = mv`

Use mass in kilograms, `kg`, velocity in metres per second, `m/s`, and momentum in `kg m/s`.
Momentum is a vector and has the same direction as velocity.
Choose a positive direction and keep velocity and momentum signs throughout a collision calculation.

## Conservation of momentum

In an isolated system, total momentum before an interaction equals total momentum after it.

`total momentum before = total momentum after`

Add momenta as signed vectors.
Momentum can be conserved even when kinetic energy is not conserved, so do not assume every collision is elastic.

## Impulse and force

Impulse equals the change in momentum:

`impulse = F x change in time = change in momentum`

For an average resultant force:

`F = change in momentum / change in time`

Impulse has units `N s`, equivalent to `kg m/s`.
Force is measured in newtons, so force and impulse are not the same quantity.

When initial and final momentum are fixed, increasing the stopping time does not change the momentum change.
It reduces the average force.
This explains the permitted crash-structure example, where crushing extends stopping time, and the rubber-mallet comparison, where contact time is longer for the same momentum change.
