# Orbits and the Solar System

The Solar System contains the Sun and objects held in orbit by its gravity.
A **planet** orbits the Sun, a **moon** is a natural satellite orbiting another body, and an **artificial satellite** is an object placed in orbit by people.

Gravity is the attractive force between masses.
For a satellite in a circular orbit, gravity provides the inward **centripetal force**, which is the resultant force directed towards the centre of the circle.
There is no separate outward force balancing gravity in an inertial description of the orbit.

The satellite's speed can remain constant while its velocity changes because velocity includes direction.
This continuous change in velocity means the satellite is accelerating towards the centre even at constant speed.
It is continually falling under gravity while its sideways motion carries it around the central body rather than directly into it.

Gravity does not disappear in orbit.
Artificial satellites can support timing, mapping and logistics, while satellite-navigation systems use signals timed by orbiting clocks.
