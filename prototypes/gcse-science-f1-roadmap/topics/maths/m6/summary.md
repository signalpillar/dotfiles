## Straight-line graphs

A straight-line relationship can be written as `y = mx + c`.
The gradient `m` is the change in `y` divided by the change in `x`, and the intercept `c` is the value of `y` where `x = 0`.

- Choose two well-separated points on the line and calculate `gradient = change in y / change in x`.
- Include units by dividing the vertical-axis unit by the horizontal-axis unit.
- Interpret both the gradient and intercept in the context of the graph rather than reporting numbers alone.

On a distance-time graph, a rise from `0 m` to `450 m` in `5 s` has gradient `450 / 5 = 90 m/s`, so the gradient represents speed.
For a curve, draw a tangent at the required point and calculate the gradient of that straight tangent to estimate the gradient at that instant.
