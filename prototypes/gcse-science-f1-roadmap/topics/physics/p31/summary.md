# Stopping distance and braking

Total stopping distance is the sum of thinking distance and braking distance.
Thinking distance is travelled during the driver's reaction time, before braking begins.
At constant speed, `thinking distance = speed x reaction time`, so it increases with either speed or reaction time.

Braking distance is travelled after the brakes begin to act.
It depends on initial speed, braking force, tyre and road conditions, vehicle load, and other interacting factors.
Greater speed means much more kinetic energy must be transferred during braking, so braking distance does not generally rise in simple direct proportion to speed.

Thinking distance and braking distance are not the same and need not scale in the same way.
Grip and stopping distance cannot be predicted from one factor alone.
Published stopping-distance tables are guidance based on assumptions, not universal values for every vehicle and condition.
