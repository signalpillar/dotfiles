# Lenses, colour and total internal reflection

A converging lens refracts parallel rays so that they meet at the principal focus.
In a ray diagram, a ray parallel to the principal axis refracts through the far focus, while a ray through the optical centre continues straight in the thin-lens model.
The point where refracted rays meet locates a real image; where backward extensions of diverging rays appear to meet locates a virtual image.

For an object beyond the focal length, a converging lens forms a real, inverted image that can be projected onto a screen.
For an object inside the focal length, it forms an upright, enlarged virtual image that cannot be projected.
An object beyond twice the focal length produces a diminished, inverted, real image between one and two focal lengths on the other side.
Real does not mean upright.

A colour filter transmits a selected range of visible wavelengths and absorbs other components of the incident light.
It does not add colour that was absent from the incoming light.

Total internal reflection occurs only when light travels from a higher refractive index material towards a lower refractive index material and the incidence angle is greater than the critical angle.
Fibre-optic links guide signals using total internal reflection.
On-board cameras use converging lenses, while a magnifying glass can explain either a virtual enlarged image or a focused real bright spot; the focused-light context is explanatory, not a practical instruction.
