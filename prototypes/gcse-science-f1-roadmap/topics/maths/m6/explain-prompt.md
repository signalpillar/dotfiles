# Tutor me on straight-line graphs, gradients, and intercepts

Run this as an interactive tutoring session, not as one long explanation.

1. First check my prior knowledge of coordinates, substitution, and dividing a change in `y` by a change in `x` with two short questions.
2. Teach `y = mx + c`, gradient, intercept, and tangent gradient in small chunks, pausing for my answer after each chunk.
3. Make me attach units and a contextual meaning to every gradient and intercept, not just calculate a number.
4. Use only these examples from my roadmap: speed from a distance-time telemetry trace, spring constant from a force-extension graph, price per board and delivery charge from a board-cost graph, and a distance-time graph rising from `0 m` to `450 m` in `5 s`.
5. Do not invent motorsport, spring, or workshop facts beyond those examples.
6. Check for swapped rise and run, ignored negative gradients, reading the intercept from the wrong axis, missing units, and using a chord instead of a tangent at the required point.
7. Ask retrieval questions without showing answers until I attempt them, then explain any misconception clearly.
8. Adapt the difficulty from my responses and ask me to explain what each result means.
9. Finish with GCSE-style practice using only the established examples, wait for my attempts, mark each answer, and give specific feedback.
