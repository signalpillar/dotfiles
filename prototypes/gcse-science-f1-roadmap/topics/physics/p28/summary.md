# Red-shift and the Big Bang

Atoms produce characteristic patterns of spectral lines.
Astronomers compare the known pattern with light from a distant galaxy.
If the same pattern is shifted towards longer wavelengths, it is **red-shifted**; this does not simply mean that the galaxy looks red.

Light from many distant galaxies is red-shifted, showing that those galaxies are generally receding from us.
The overall pattern supports the conclusion that the universe is expanding.
One shifted object alone would not be complete evidence for that conclusion.

The **cosmic microwave background**, or CMB, is microwave radiation observed throughout space.
It is interpreted as cooled radiation from an earlier hot, dense universe and is evidence related to the Big Bang model.

The Big Bang is a scientific model supported by observations, not a claim of an ordinary explosion from one centre into pre-existing space.
Laboratory or materials spectrometers and a workshop spectroscope separate light by wavelength; astronomers use the same underlying idea when comparing spectral-line patterns from galaxies.
