# Measuring density

Density describes how much mass is contained in a given volume.

`density = mass / volume`

Use compatible units throughout.
Mass in grams with volume in cubic centimetres gives density in `g/cm^3`; mass in kilograms with volume in cubic metres gives `kg/m^3`.
Do not mix the two systems within one substitution.

For a regular solid, measure its dimensions, calculate its volume from its shape, measure its mass, and divide mass by volume.
For an irregular solid, its volume is the final water reading minus the initial water reading, not either reading alone.
For a liquid, use its measured mass and measured volume, keeping their units compatible.

Mass and density are different quantities.
Equal masses of different materials can occupy different volumes because their densities differ.
