# Transformers and electromagnetic induction

Electromagnetic induction occurs when the magnetic flux linking a conductor changes, producing an induced potential difference.
A generator creates this change through relative motion between a conductor and a magnetic field, transferring mechanical energy electrically.

A transformer has primary and secondary coils linked by a changing magnetic flux in a core.
An alternating input is needed to maintain the changing flux.
For an ideal transformer:

`V_p / V_s = N_p / N_s`

`V_p I_p = V_s I_s`

Potential differences `V_p` and `V_s` are in volts, currents `I_p` and `I_s` in amperes, and turns `N_p` and `N_s` are counts.
A greater secondary-to-primary turns ratio steps potential difference up; a smaller ratio steps it down.
Do not swap primary and secondary quantities midway through a calculation.

The power relationship is an ideal model.
A step-up transformer increases potential difference while current decreases; it does not step up ideal power or create energy.
Real transformers transfer slightly less output power because some energy is dissipated.

In a moving-coil microphone, sound moves a diaphragm and attached coil relative to a magnetic field.
The changing flux induces a varying electrical signal corresponding to the sound vibration.
This explanation applies specifically to moving-coil microphones; other microphone designs use different transduction mechanisms.
A moving-coil microphone acts as a generator, not a motor, because movement produces the electrical signal.
