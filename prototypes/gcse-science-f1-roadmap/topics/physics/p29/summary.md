# Nuclear fission and fusion

**Nuclear fission** is the splitting of a large, unstable nucleus into smaller nuclei, usually after it absorbs a neutron.
The reaction transfers energy and releases more neutrons.
If a released neutron causes another fission, the repeated process is a chain reaction.
This neutron-driven chain reaction is not fusion and is different from spontaneous radioactive decay.

In a controlled fission reactor, energy transferred by fission can ultimately contribute to electricity generation.
This supports the limited statement that some national grids, and therefore some household or facility electricity, may include fission generation; it does not establish the source of any particular supply or justify broad reactor-safety claims.

**Nuclear fusion** joins two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus and transfers energy.
Positively charged nuclei repel each other, so fusion requires extreme temperature to give nuclei enough kinetic energy and extreme pressure or confinement to make collisions sufficiently likely.
Fusion powers the Sun, not an ordinary F1 power unit.

Fission splits heavy nuclei and can sustain a neutron chain reaction.
Fusion joins light nuclei and requires extreme conditions.
