# Forces, scalars and vectors

A scalar has magnitude only.
A vector has magnitude and direction.
Force is a vector, so a force description is incomplete without its direction.
Use consistent units before comparing or combining magnitudes.

Contact forces require objects to touch and include friction, normal reaction, tension, drag and an applied push or pull.
Non-contact forces act across a distance and include gravitational, magnetic and electrostatic forces.

## Free-body diagrams

A free-body diagram isolates one object and shows every external force acting on that object.

- Draw the object as a simple box or dot.
- Draw one labelled arrow for each force, pointing in the force's direction.
- Use arrow length to indicate relative magnitude when that information is known.
- Keep distinct forces separate, even when they point in the same direction.
- Do not add an arrow merely to show motion.
- Do not draw a third-law partner that acts on a different object.

For the straight-line car context, tyre force acts forwards and drag backwards.
Weight, aerodynamic downforce and normal reaction are three distinct vertical forces.
The diagram must show the individual forces rather than replace them with an arrow for the car's motion.

Equal-size pushes and pulls on a stuck bolt are different vectors when their directions differ.
