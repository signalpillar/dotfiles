# Mains electricity and safety

An alternating supply repeatedly reverses the direction of current and the polarity of the potential difference.
This differs from a direct supply with one polarity.

In a mains circuit, the live conductor provides an alternating potential difference relative to earth and the neutral conductor completes the normal circuit.
Neutral is near earth potential in normal operation, but it must never be assumed harmless.
The earth conductor is a safety path connected to exposed metal cases.
It normally carries no operating current; during a fault it can provide a low-resistance path so a large current causes the protective device to disconnect the supply.

A fuse contains a wire that melts when excessive current heats it, opening the circuit.
A circuit breaker is a resettable protective switch that opens a circuit when its operating condition is exceeded.
These devices protect wiring against excessive current, but neither makes every electrical hazard safe.
Double insulation is an alternative protective design that does not rely on an exposed metal case being earthed.

Electrical power is the rate of energy transfer:

`P = VI`

Power `P` is measured in watts (`W`), potential difference `V` in volts (`V`), and current `I` in amperes (`A`).

Protected garage mains circuits and a car's separately isolated high-voltage system are different systems with different controls.
A metal-cased drill can be designed with an earth path or with double insulation.
These are explanations of protective principles, not instructions for inspecting, wiring, or repairing electrical equipment.
