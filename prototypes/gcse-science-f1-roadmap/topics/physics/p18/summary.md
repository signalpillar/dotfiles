# Gas pressure and temperature

The kinetic particle model treats a gas as many particles moving rapidly and randomly.
Gas pressure is caused by particles colliding with container walls and changing momentum, which produces a force over the wall area.
This is a simplified model, not a literal picture of particle size or paths.

For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume.
Reducing the volume makes wall collisions more frequent, so pressure rises.

For a fixed mass at constant volume, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature.
For a fixed mass at constant pressure, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature.
These relationships use kelvin, not degrees Celsius.

Always state what is held constant.
A change in temperature does not by itself tell you whether pressure, volume, or both will change.
Pressure is not material stored inside a gas; it describes the effect of particle collisions.
