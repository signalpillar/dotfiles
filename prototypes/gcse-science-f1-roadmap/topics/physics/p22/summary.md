# The electromagnetic spectrum

Electromagnetic waves form one continuous spectrum.
In order of increasing frequency, it is radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.
Wavelength decreases as frequency increases.

All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum: approximately `3.0 x 10^8 m/s`.
Different regions do not have different vacuum speeds.
Their different frequencies and wavelengths affect how they interact with matter and therefore their uses and hazards.

Team radio uses radio waves; Wi-Fi uses microwaves; thermal cameras and TV remotes use infrared; work lights and inspection cameras use visible light; and X-rays can produce medical images or examine hidden composite damage.
These uses do not prove that exposure is harmless.
Ultraviolet can damage cells, while X-rays and gamma rays are ionising and can also damage cells.
Radio, microwave, infrared, and visible radiation are not all ionising simply because they are electromagnetic radiation.
