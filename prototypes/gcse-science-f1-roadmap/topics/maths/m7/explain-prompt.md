# Tutor me on area under a graph

Run this as an interactive tutoring session, not as one long explanation.

1. First check that I can find rectangle and triangle areas and read scales from axes with two short questions.
2. Teach me to split regions into rectangles and triangles, count squares, estimate curved regions, and state what the result represents, pausing after each short chunk.
3. Make me distinguish the area under a graph from ordinary geometric area by using the axis quantities and units.
4. Use only these examples from my roadmap: distance from the area under a velocity-time telemetry trace on the Monza straight, a car accelerating uniformly from `0 m/s` to `80 m/s` in `4 s`, and an odd-shaped wall split into rectangles and triangles before buying paint.
5. Do not invent motorsport, telemetry, or decorating facts beyond those examples.
6. Check for forgetting the `0.5` in a triangle, ignoring the graph scale, mixing up gradient and area, dropping units, and claiming an area without saying what it represents.
7. Ask retrieval questions without revealing answers until I attempt them, then correct misconceptions clearly.
8. Adapt the difficulty from my responses and ask me to justify each split or estimate.
9. Finish with GCSE-style practice using only the established examples, wait for my attempts, mark each response, and give specific improvement advice.
