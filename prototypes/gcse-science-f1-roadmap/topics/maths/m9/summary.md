## Vectors and resolving forces

A vector has both magnitude and direction, while a scalar has magnitude only.
Represent a vector with an arrow whose length follows a chosen scale and whose direction follows the arrowhead.

Add vectors graphically by placing them head to tail and drawing the resultant from the first tail to the final head.
For perpendicular components, use trigonometry to resolve a vector and Pythagoras to recover its magnitude:

- A component adjacent to angle `theta` has magnitude `F cos(theta)`.
- A component opposite angle `theta` has magnitude `F sin(theta)`.
- Perpendicular components `Fx` and `Fy` give `F = sqrt(Fx^2 + Fy^2)`.

Do not add magnitudes alone when directions differ.
In the traction-circle model, when the tyre is operating at the boundary of the available total grip, braking and cornering are perpendicular components and Pythagoras gives the maximum available cornering component for the stated braking component.
