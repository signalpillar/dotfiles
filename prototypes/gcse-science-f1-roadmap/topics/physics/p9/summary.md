# Pressure, density and fluids

Pressure is normal force per unit area:

`p = F / A`

Pressure `p` is measured in pascals (`Pa`), where `1 Pa = 1 N/m^2`.
For the same normal force, a smaller area produces a greater pressure, so pressure is not the same quantity as force.

Density is mass per unit volume:

`density = mass / volume`

The SI unit of density is `kg/m^3`.
Keep mass and volume units compatible and do not mix density units without conversion.

In a fluid at rest, pressure increases with depth because of the fluid above.
Atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude but does not suddenly become zero.
Fluid pressure, density, force, and area are related ideas, but each remains a distinct quantity.
