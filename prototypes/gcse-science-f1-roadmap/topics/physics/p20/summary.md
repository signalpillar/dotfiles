# Static electricity and electric fields

Rubbing two insulating materials can transfer electrons from one to the other.
An object that gains electrons becomes negatively charged, while one that loses electrons becomes positively charged.
Protons remain bound in atomic nuclei and are not transferred between the objects.

Like net charges repel and opposite net charges attract.
Attraction alone does not prove that two objects have opposite net charges because a charged object can attract a neutral object after charges within it redistribute.

A spark is a rapid discharge through the surrounding material when built-up charge moves.
Earthing provides a path for electrons to move between an object and Earth until the excess charge is removed; charge is transferred, not destroyed.

An electric field is the region where a charge experiences a force.
Its conventional direction is the direction of force on a positive test charge: away from positive charge and toward negative charge.
Field lines are a representation of the field, not physical threads.

Bonding and earthing around fuel handling are professional safety controls intended to prevent a spark near vapour.
This context must be explained, never attempted as a learner activity.
