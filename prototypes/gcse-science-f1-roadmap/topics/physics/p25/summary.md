# Energy resources and the National Grid

A renewable resource is replenished naturally on a human timescale, although its available flow may vary.
A non-renewable resource is finite or is replaced too slowly to match its use.
Renewable does not mean continuously available, impact-free, or automatically sustainable.

Compare energy resources using the needs in the question, such as reliability, availability, cost, and environmental effects.
Do not decide from one label alone.
For example, rooftop solar with grid backup involves a trade between intermittent local generation and reliable supply from the wider grid.
Race facilities likewise need dependable electricity for garages, timing, and broadcasting while different supply options can be compared by reliability and emissions.

The National Grid transfers electrical power from generators to consumers.
For a fixed transmitted power:

`P = VI`

Increasing potential difference `V` reduces current `I`.
Heating power lost in cables is related to:

`P_loss = I^2 R`

For the same cable resistance `R`, a lower current therefore produces much less heating loss.
Step-up transformers increase the alternating potential difference before long-distance transmission, and step-down transformers reduce it for later distribution.
Transformers transfer energy; they do not create it.
Real transformers and cables are not perfectly efficient, so this is a useful model rather than a claim of zero loss.
