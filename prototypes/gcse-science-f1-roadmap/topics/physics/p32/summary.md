# Drag and terminal velocity

Drag is a force that opposes motion through a fluid such as a liquid or gas.
Its size depends on conditions including speed, fluid density, area, and shape, so drag should not be treated as constant.

For a falling object released from rest, weight acts downward and is approximately constant while drag starts small.
The downward resultant force causes acceleration, but as speed and drag increase, the resultant force and acceleration decrease.
At terminal velocity, upward drag equals downward weight.
The resultant force and acceleration are then zero, but both forces still act and the object continues at constant velocity.

Acceleration falls because the resultant force falls, not because the object's mass changes.
The same resultant-force reasoning applies when driving force is opposed by increasing drag: acceleration decreases as the forces approach balance.
